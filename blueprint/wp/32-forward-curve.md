## 1. Why

WP 31 L6 (decision D1) prices each product on a flat rate and yield fitted to the market's discount DF(T) and forward F(T) at its last date T. A vanilla expiring at T then reprices exactly under every model; a product with earlier dates (an autocall's observations, an Asian's fixings, a barrier) sees the carry flat over its life. The market is not flat:
- SPX's implied rate runs from 4.4 % at two months to 5.1 % at one to two years (WP 31 §4.7);
- SPY's December dividend (1.9 $) is a jump in its forward, which a flat yield spreads over the year;
- a single stock's discrete dividends are the same.

Every model today takes a scalar `rate` and `dividend`: the CPU simulation models through `ScriptModelSpec`, the GPU and JIT kernels (`dm.r`, `dm.q`), the AAD adjoints (one rho, one dividend risk) and the analytic engines. Only the Hull-White equity hybrid reads a discount curve. New models are planned; adding the curve to each one after the fact would be a retrofit per model. This WP defines the one object they all read, before they land.

## 2. Design

- **`ForwardCurve`** (`include/quantModeling/market/forward_curve.hpp`): the spot S0, a `DiscountCurve` (the existing class, flat-forward extrapolation), and ln(F(t)/S0) on pillars, linear in t between them (a piecewise-constant carry rate) and flat-carry beyond. It answers `forward(t)`, `discount(t)`, `carry(t1, t2) = ln F(t2)/F(t1)` and `rate(t1, t2) = ln DF(t1)/DF(t2)`. A flat curve is built from (r, q) and reproduces today's numbers bit for bit: that is F1's first test.
- **The models' contract.** `ScriptModelSpec` carries an optional `ForwardCurve`; absent, the flat one of (rate, dividend). A model's step uses `carry(t_i, t_i+1)` where it used (r − q) Δt, and the script engine discounts a payment at t by `discount(t)`. The local-vol grid maps k to K through F(t) (WP 31 makes k forward moneyness already).
- **Discrete dividends.** A later extension: a pillar pair around an ex-date makes the carry a jump; the curve's linear-in-t log-forward handles it with two pillars a second apart. No proportional-versus-cash choice is made here.
- **AAD.** Risks per pillar: rho per discount pillar, a carry risk per forward pillar; today's scalar rho and dividend risk are their sums, so the response keeps its fields.
- **GPU and JIT.** The host precomputes, on the simulation's time grid, one carry per step and one discount per payment date, as device arrays: the kernels read `carry[step]` instead of `(r - q) * dt`. One change, the same in every kernel; the JIT kernel reads the same arrays.

## 4. As built

### F1 (2026-11-27)

- `include/quantModeling/market/forward_curve.hpp`, `src/market/forward_curve.cpp`; `qm.ForwardCurve` in the Python module (`bind_market.cpp`), built from (spot, times, forwards, discount factors) or `ForwardCurve.flat(spot, rate, dividend)`.
- **The flat curve keeps its scalars.** `flat(S0, r, q)` stores (r, q) and evaluates the models' own expressions: `forward(t) = S0 e^{(r − q) t}`, `discount(t) = e^{−r t}`, `carry(t1, t2) = (r − q)(t2 − t1)`. Bit for bit with those expressions is tested. It does not make a model's price bit for bit on its own: several models fold the drift into one constant, `(r − q − σ²/2) Δt`, which `carry − σ²Δt/2` does not reproduce to the last bit. So F2 keeps each model's scalar path when `is_flat()`, and reads the curve otherwise; `flat_rate()` and `flat_dividend()` hand the scalars back.
- `dividend(t1, t2) = rate − carry`, the integrated dividend yield (repo included): the leg a model with a separate rate and yield (a numeraire, a quanto drift) needs.
- `log_forward_weights(t)` gives ln F(t) as a combination of the pillars' ln F, as `DiscountCurve::log_discount_weights` does for ln DF: the risks per forward pillar of F3 are built on it.
- `tests/testForwardCurve.cpp`: the flat curve's expressions bit for bit; pillars repriced; carry and rate telescoping over any grid, extrapolation included; zero coupons reprice DF(t) and F·DF = S0 e^{−∫q}; E[S_t] = F(t) under exact lognormal steps on a grid that ignores the pillars (200,000 paths, 4 standard errors); a cash dividend as two pillars a second apart, exact away from the gap; the weights against pillar bumps.

### F2 (2026-11-27)

- **The contract.** `ISimulationModel::set_forward_curve(curve)`, before `init()`; `ScriptModelSpec::forward_curve` (optional) is read by `make_script_model`. Black-Scholes, local vol, Heston (and Bates), SLV and rough Bergomi take it. The several-underlying models, the Hull-White hybrid and the short-rate models refuse a curve with an explicit error: a multi-asset curve needs one forward per asset over one discount curve, left for when a multi-asset product needs it.
- **A flat curve is its scalars.** The factory turns `ForwardCurve::flat(S0, r, q)` back into (r, q) and today's path: every model's paths are bit for bit those of the scalars (tested), and the device route is unchanged.
- **Rate and dividend become parallel shifts.** On a curve the model's `rate` and `div` parameters are zero and shift the curve: step k's rate argument is `c_k + r` with `c_k = ln F(t_k)/F(t_{k−1}) / Δt_k`, the numeraire `e^{r t}/DF(t)`, a discount `DF(m)/DF(t) e^{−r(m−t)}`, a forward `S F(m)/F(t) e^{(r−q)(m−t)}`. So the AAD rho and dividend risk are those of a parallel shift of the curve (tested against `ForwardCurve::shifted`), the sums the per-pillar risks of F3 will split. The bumped rho of `script_route` bumps the curve the same way (`shifted(±h, 0)`).
- **No curve lookup on a path.** `CurveCarry` (`models/equity/curve_carry.hpp`) tabulates in `init()` the carry rate of every step and, per event, 1/DF, the discount ratios and the forward growths a payoff reads. The step functors shared with the IR (`path_steps.hpp`) are unchanged: they receive `c_k + r` where they received `r`.
- **CPU only until F4.** `describe_device` refuses a model on a curve, so the GPU, the compiled IR and the JIT decline it and the engine stays on the CPU (an explicit GPU request says why).
- **Set aside here.** A route's `control_mean` is a closed form on the scalars: it is not used on a curve (F6). Black-Scholes's Brownian-bridge survival over a step that contains a pillar assumes the step's average carry, an approximation of second order (the carry's change across a pillar is under 1 %/yr). Theta by bumped dates keeps the curve anchored to today.
- `tests/testForwardCurveModels.cpp`, under every model: a flat curve bit for bit; numeraire, discounts and forwards are the curve's (zero coupons reprice DF(t)); E[S(t)/N(t)] = F(t) DF(t) at dates around the pillars and the dividend, which drops the deflated spot by its amount; where the drift does not feed the vol (Black-Scholes, Heston, rough Bergomi) a vanilla on the curve equals, path by path, the vanilla under the flat (r, q) fitted at its maturity, to 1e-11; Black-Scholes against Black's formula and Heston against its COS price on F(T), DF(T); the AAD rate and dividend against the shifted curve; a script through `simulate_script`.

### F3 (2026-11-27)

- **Discounting on DF(t) came with F2.** A script's payments are deflated by the model's numeraire, `e^{r t}/DF(t)` on a curve, and `df(T)` reads the model's discounts: nothing in the script engine discounts on its own, so F3 had nothing more to change there.
- **The pillar risks are parameters, not a map after the run.** As the Hull-White hybrid's `CurveFactors` (and for its reason: each risk keeps its own Monte-Carlo error), every pillar has a shift, a model parameter at zero, appended after the model's own: `zero[t_j]` moves the zero rate of the discount pillar at t_j, and the forward with it as the scalar rate does; `div_yield[t_i]` moves the dividend yield of the forward pillar at t_i. Through the curve's own interpolation weights, Δ ln DF(t) = −Σ w_j(t) t_j δ_j and Δ ln F(t) = Σ w_j(t) t_j δ_j − Σ u_i(t) t_i ε_i. Both interpolations reproduce a linear function of t (log-linear from the origin, a flat forward past the last pillar), so equal shifts are the parallel shift and the pillars sum to the scalar `rate` and `div`, which stay. A rate pillar is a pillar of the discount curve: a `ForwardCurve` on a flat-rate discount curve has none.
- **Sparse on the tape.** The weights are local: each step and each event reads two to four pillars. `CurveCarry` tabulates their coefficients once (in each step's rate argument, each event's log numeraire, discount ratios and forward growths), and `ModelCurve<T>` adds them on the tape only: with T = Real nothing is read and the paths are F2's, bit for bit.
- **Labels.** `zero[t]` (the Hull-White hybrid's name for a pillar's zero rate) and `div_yield[t]`, t the pillar's time in years to ten significant digits, so that a dividend's two pillars a second apart stay apart (indices first, renamed in F5: `div[i]` is a multi-asset model's dividend of asset i). `script_route` maps only the exact label `rate` to rho, so the pillars do not double count.
- `tests/testForwardCurveModels.cpp`: under every model, the pathwise risk to each pillar against the bumped pillar (1e-6) and the pillars' sums against the scalar rho and dividend risk (1e-10); through `simulate_aad` on a script, the same sums, no risk to the pillars past the script's dates, and the same bits on four threads as on one.

### F5 (2026-11-27)

- **What the market hands over.** `chain_iv.MarketCurve` reads a ticker's accepted `options.chain_iv_expiries` (21 to 54 expiries on 2026-11-26: SPX from 2 days to 4.07 years, SPY to 2.15): each expiry's forward, carried onto the close as `CarryCurve` does (F S / S_expiry), and its discount factor. A typed rate replaces the discounts, flat, and keeps the forwards (WP 31's override). `LocalVolMarket.curve` holds it under `chain_iv` only; legacy has none and is unchanged.
- **Who reads it.** `price_script` (`forward_times`, `forwards`, `forward_dfs`) for black_scholes, local_vol, heston, slv and rough_bergomi on one underlying; the superbucket's repricing. A closed form (F6), several underlyings and Hull-White keep WP 31 L6's flat carry at the product's horizon, which `LocalVolMarket.rate` and `dividend` still are.
- **The calibrations sit on it, and stop depending on the horizon.** The Dupire grid placed its strikes at the flat forward of the horizon fit, K = S e^{(r−q)T} e^k: a smile at the wrong strikes at every maturity but the horizon. It now places them at the curve's forwards (`calibrate_vol_surface`, `build_local_vol_grid`, the bands, both superbuckets: an optional `ForwardCurve`; none is the flat one, bit for bit). SLV's particles step on the curve's carry (`calibrate_slv_leverage`). Heston reads only the slices' moneyness. So the surface's cache key and the stochastic-vol store key take the curve instead of the horizon's (rate, dividend): one calibration serves every product on a snapshot, where L6 calibrated once per horizon.
- **The local-vol pricing route** (`/api/local-vol/price`, a flat-carry engine) fits its carry at the option's maturity instead of one year, so its paths end on the curve's forward where the grid puts the smile.
- **Audit and replay.** A valuation records `forward_curve:<ticker>`. A replay now compares only the inputs both runs read: an input read on one side only is a change of code, which the code builds tell, not a revised datum (a valuation recorded before F5 would otherwise replay as `drifted_inputs`).
- **The page.** The pillar risks are renamed `zero[t]` and `div_yield[t]` (§F3). The greeks table keeps Rho and the dividend risk; a "Rho and dividend by expiry" block lists the expiries the product reads, both risks per row.
- **Set aside.** On a curve every simulation ran on the CPU until F4, which ships right after.
- `api/tests/test_forward_curve.py` (a synthetic chain whose rate and yield rise with the maturity): the curve from the expiries; one surface for every horizon, its strikes at the curve's forwards; an observation at three months reads the market's forward under black_scholes, local_vol, heston and slv, where the flat fit at two years misses it; a typed rate; the pillar risks summing to rho and the dividend risk, a zero coupon's on its own pillar; the replay rule. `tests/testForwardCurveModels.cpp`: the grid and the leverage on a flat curve bit for bit, on a curve independent of the scalars.

### F4 (2026-11-27)

- **The device describes the curve.** `DeviceModel::curve` and `step_carry` (each step's carry rate, the CPU model's `CurveCarry` table): Black-Scholes, local vol, Heston and SLV describe themselves on a curve instead of declining the GPU.
- **Interpreted kernels** (`script_path.hpp`): each step's rate is c_k + r for local vol, Heston (Euler and QE) and SLV (Euler and QE); Black-Scholes reads the drift its CPU model tabulated. The event tables (numeraire, discounts) are the curve's, computed on the host with the CPU model's expressions (`mc::curve_event_tables`) instead of the device's flat `event_tables_kernel`.
- **Kernels compiled from the IR** (`ir_path.cpp`, `PathLayout::curve`): a step column `carry_col` added to the rate theta, for every model of a risk program and local vol, Heston and SLV of a price one. A price's date rows hold the curve's factors times the shift's, computed on the host; a risk's keep the times and maturities and add the curve's factors from `factor_col0`, which `Num`, `Df` and `Fwd` multiply in. The JIT compiles the IR, so it follows: its source changes with the layout, and so its cache key. Multilevel's levels each describe their own grid's carry.
- **Risks.** The tangent (Black-Scholes, Heston), the per-path adjoint (local vol, SLV), Vibrato and the IR's CPU adjoint take the rate and the dividend as parallel shifts, as the tape does. The pillars are not thetas: a device run's labels are the model's own (`device_risk_labels`), and its diagnostics say that the risks per expiry run on the CPU's tape. On the site that means Black-Scholes's greeks (CPU) show Rho by expiry, Heston's, SLV's and multilevel local vol's (GPU) the parallel Rho only.
- **Set aside:** the pillars' risks on the GPU. Each step and event reads two to four pillars, so they could be thetas with sparse per-step coefficients in the rows; not done here.
- `tests/gpu/testGpuForwardCurve.cpp`: CPU against GPU on a curve with a cash dividend, within 1e-12 on prices (compiled, Sobol and pseudo-random; interpreted; Euler and QE; multilevel within its error), the curve reaching the kernels; the GPU's risks against the tape's within 1e-10 of the largest, and its rate and dividend the sums of the tape's pillars. `tests/testForwardCurveModels.cpp`: the IR's CPU adjoint against the tape.

### F6 (2026-11-27)

- **Closed forms on the curve** (`engines/analytic/curve_closed_forms.hpp`). Under Black-Scholes on a curve, ln S(t) is Gaussian with mean ln F(t) − σ²t/2 and covariance σ² min(s, t), whatever the carry: a closed form reads the curve only through the forwards at its dates and the discount at its payment. `black_scholes_vanilla`, `black_scholes_digital`, `equity_forward`, `geometric_asian` (exact) and `arithmetic_asian_levy` (moment matching on the curve's forwards at the fixings, an approximation) return their price and their sensitivities to those ln F(t) and ln DF(t).
- **Exact risks per pillar.** `curve_pillar_risks` maps those sensitivities through the curve's interpolation weights, as F3 does on the tape: `zero[t]` and `div_yield[t]`, labelled as the Monte Carlo's, summing to the parallel rho and dividend risk (`curve_parallel_risks`).
- **The catalog's engines keep their scalars.** `BSEuroVanillaAnalyticEngine`, the Asian and digital engines and Heston's COS take typed (r, q): a flat curve is its scalars, and nothing the API prices on the market goes through them.
- **A bias fixed: the control variates.** Under Black-Scholes on a curve, a script's `controlPays` had its expectation computed on the scalar (rate, dividend) the binding passed, which on a curve are the flat fit at the horizon: a control at an earlier date had the wrong mean, and the controlled price a bias (6.6 % on the test's product: 4.553 against 4.876). `ScriptedProduct::black_scholes_control_mean` takes the curve and reads its F(t), DF(t). The Asian route's geometric control, left off on a curve in F2, is the curve's geometric Asian.
- **The API's analytic route** (`engine='analytic'`: the European, digital and forward of the library) read its flat carry at T from Python's `CarryCurve`, whose carry *rate* is linear in T between expiries; the Monte Carlo's `ForwardCurve` makes ln F linear. Between two expiries they differed. The route now takes F(T) and DF(T) from `qm.closed_form_on_curve`, the C++ curve, and returns the closed form's risks per expiry (`risks`, zero standard error); the page shows them under the greeks table (`CurveRisks`, `exact`). `LocalVolMarket.carry_for` goes.
- `tests/testCurveClosedForms.cpp`: on a flat curve the scalar formulas; on an SPX-like curve with a cash dividend, the closed forms against `simulate_script` under Black-Scholes on the curve (the vanilla, the digital and the geometric Asian within 4 standard errors, Levy's within its approximation); every pillar's risk against the bumped pillar, their sums against the shifted curve; a `controlPays` at an earlier date unbiased on the curve's mean, biased on the flat fit's. `api/tests/test_forward_curve.py`: the analytic route between two expiries against the Monte Carlo, its rho by expiry on those two expiries summing to Rho; a typed rate.
